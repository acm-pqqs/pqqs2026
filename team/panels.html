ACM Conference on Post-Quantum and Quantum-based Security

ACM PQQS 2026

November 2–4, 2026 • San Jose, CA, USA

Panels

Panel 1: Transition to PQ Security: Challenges from Theory to Practice

Monday, November 2, 1:00–2:00 PM

Dr. Matthew Campagna

Panel Moderator

Affiliation: Amazon Web Services

Biography: Matthew Campagna is a Senior Principal Engineer and Cryptographer at Amazon Web Services (AWS), where he leads the design and analysis of cryptographic solutions. Since joining Amazon in 2013, he has spent the past decade driving AWS’s migration to post-quantum cryptography, ensuring cloud infrastructure is prepared for the next generation of computing threats.

Matthew represents Amazon in the ETSI Security Algorithms Group of Experts (SAGE) and chairs the ETSI TC CYBER Quantum Safe Cryptography working group.

Prior to Amazon, Matthew managed the Cryptography Research Group at Certicom/BlackBerry, where he led intellectual property development and standardization efforts for elliptic curve cryptography. Earlier in his career, he served as a cryptologic mathematician at the National Security Agency.

Matthew received a Ph.D. in Mathematics from Wesleyan University and studied mathematics at Fordham University.

Dr. Dustin Moody

Affiliation: National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)

Biography: Dr. Dustin Moody is a mathematician in the Computer Security Division of the National Institute of Standards and Technology. Dr. Moody leads the Post-Quantum Cryptography project at NIST. He received his Ph.D. from the University of Washington in 2009. His area of research deals with elliptic curves, and their applications in cryptography.

Dr. Michele Mosca

Affiliation: Institute for Quantum Computing, University of Waterloo; evolutionQ

Biography: Dr. Michele Mosca is a pioneer in quantum computing and cybersecurity who has helped shape the global quantum and quantum-safe technology ecosystem. He initiated the quantum computing effort at the University of Waterloo in 1999 and was a principal architect of its development into the Institute for Quantum Computing (IQC), helping establish one of the world’s leading quantum research and innovation ecosystems. He is a founding member of Perimeter Institute and co-founder and CEO of evolutionQ. His work spans foundational quantum computing research, quantum-safe cybersecurity, talent development, commercialization, standards leadership, and international policy engagement. He has helped create or grow initiatives including Open Quantum Safe, the ETSI-IQC Quantum-Safe Cryptography Conference, Quantum-Safe Canada, Quantum Industry Canada, QCSYS/QSYS, and CryptoWorks21.

Manoj Sastry

Affiliation: Intel

Biography: Manoj Sastry is a Senior Principal Engineer in security and privacy at Intel, where he leads research in post-quantum cryptography. His 30-year career in industry includes prior roles at Sequent and IBM. He has driven research in cryptography, SoC and network security, privacy, trusted platforms, and security for IoT and autonomous systems. He is an inventor on more than 200 patents issued worldwide and has published 30 papers.

Panel 2: Quantum Safe Communications

Wednesday, November 4, 4:10–5:10 PM. A focus on PQC and quantum-resistance for 5G/6G, telecom, wireless, and end-to-end communication.

Dr. Jaideep Vaidya

Moderator and Panelist

Affiliation: Rutgers University

Yolanda Sanz

Affiliation: GSMA

Dr. Tao Wan

Affiliation: CableLabs

Biography: Tao Wan is a Distinguished Technologist at CableLabs, the innovation and R&D lab for the global cable and broadband industry, where he leads research on the security and privacy of wireline and wireless networks, including Wi-Fi and 4G, 5G, and 6G. His current work also includes post-quantum cryptography migration across the cable and mobile industries. He is an active contributor to 3GPP SA3 (Security and Privacy) and the GSMA Network Roaming Group (NRG). He co-chairs the Next Generation Mobile Systems Security Advisory Committee (NGSAC), a group of academic and industry security experts that aims to turn research findings into practical input for standards development. He has co-authored more than 30 papers and twice received the Network and Distributed System Security Symposium (NDSS) Distinguished Paper Award (2016 and 2026). His research on mobile network security has also been recognized by the GSMA. He holds a PhD in Computer Science from Carleton University.

Dr. Cristina Nita-Rotaru

Affiliation: Northeastern University

Dr. Imtiaz Karim

Affiliation: The University of Texas at Dallas

Biography: Imtiaz Karim is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at The University of Texas at Dallas, where he leads the System and Network Security (SysNetS) lab. He received his Ph.D. from Purdue University. His research targets the security and privacy of next-generation intelligent systems and networks, cellular (6G and beyond), transportation, and IoT, combining formal methods, program analysis, software testing, and AI to build systematic, scalable tools that uncover vulnerabilities in widely deployed protocols before they are exploited. His work has led to changes in the 4G and 5G standards and has earned acknowledgments from GSMA Mobile Security Research and the Wi-Fi Alliance. He is an Associate Editor of IEEE Transactions on Dependable and Secure Computing and a member of the National Center for Transportation Cybersecurity and Resiliency (TraCR), the Next Generation Mobile Systems Security Advisory Committee, and GSMA Open Telco AI.

Dr. Attila A. Yavuz

Affiliation: University of South Florida

Biography: Dr. Attila A. Yavuz is an internationally recognized scholar in applied cryptography and cybersecurity, bridging rigorous cryptographic design with deployable systems for post-quantum security, privacy, and mission-critical networks. As Director of the Applied Cryptography Research Laboratory and Co-Director of the Cryptologic Center at the University of South Florida, he leads a research program spanning lightweight and post-quantum authentication, privacy-enhancing technologies, and security protocols for IoT, 5G/NextG, and critical systems. His record includes over 125 publications and patents, $5.86M in competitively awarded funding ($2.25M personal share), technology transfer activity with Bosch and DOE-affiliated initiatives, and mentorship of graduates who have gone on to tenure-track positions. His service as Associate Editor of IEEE TDSC, NSF panelist, Program Co-Chair, and invited speaker at national venues and international forums reflects sustained external recognition and leadership in applied cryptography and cybersecurity.
